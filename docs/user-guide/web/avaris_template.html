Introduction to Avaris CS

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Software Introduction

Avaris CS is a professional, high-precision dynamic hydrological-hydraulic-water quality integrated simulation software designed specifically for urban drainage systems and watershed water management. It focuses on simulating the entire process of dynamic rainfall-runoff generation, confluence, and transportation, serving as a comprehensive simulation tool that integrates hydrological analysis, hydraulic calculation, and water quality evaluation. The software is applicable to both single-event rainfall-runoff simulations (e.g., extreme rainstorm events) and long-term continuous simulations (e.g., multi-year rainfall series analysis), enabling accurate prediction of urban runoff quantity and quality changes under various scenarios. Moreover, it supports seamless coupling with two-dimensional (2D) surface water models, realizing visualized simulation and quantitative analysis of urban flood inundation processes—providing reliable technical support for urban flood control, drainage system optimization, and water environment management.

Software Functions

  • Flood Risk Management & Planning: Compile high-precision urban flood risk maps with clear classification of risk levels (high, medium, low) to support scientific decision-making for urban flood control and drainage planning. It can simulate flood evolution processes under different rainfall frequencies, helping planners optimize the layout of drainage systems and formulate targeted flood control strategies.

  • Digital Twin & Smart Drainage: Provide strong technical support for the construction of urban flood control digital twins and smart drainage systems. It can integrate real-time monitoring data (e.g., rainfall, water level, flow rate) to realize dynamic updating and visual display of the drainage system's operation status, laying a foundation for intelligent scheduling and early warning of waterlogging.

  • Drainage Capacity & Waterlogging Risk Analysis: Evaluate the drainage capacity of urban pipe networks, culverts, and pumping stations comprehensively, identify bottlenecks in the drainage system (e.g., insufficient pipe diameter, low pumping capacity), and conduct quantitative analysis of flooding risk in key areas (e.g., low-lying roads, residential districts). It can also simulate the duration, depth, and scope of waterlogging to provide a basis for flooding control.

  • Facility Scale Optimization & Water Quality Improvement: Conduct quantitative analysis on the scale and performance of flooding control and water quality improvement facilities (e.g., pumping stations, detention basins, sewage treatment plants). It can optimize the design parameters of facilities to ensure their effectiveness in reducing waterlogging and improving water quality, while balancing engineering investment and operational efficiency.

  • Combined Sewer Overflow (CSO) Pollution Control: Simulate the generation mechanism and pollution characteristics of combined sewer overflow under rainfall conditions, quantify the overflow volume, pollutant concentration (e.g., COD, BOD, SS), and pollution load. It provides technical support for formulating CSO control measures (e.g., overflow interception, storage tanks) and improving the water quality of receiving water bodies.

  • Green Infrastructure Evaluation: Evaluate the effectiveness of green infrastructure (e.g., green roofs, permeable pavements, rain gardens) in controlling runoff quantity and improving runoff quality. It can simulate the retention, infiltration, and purification effects of green infrastructure on rainfall runoff, providing a basis for the planning and layout of urban sponge city construction.

  • Non-point Source Pollution & TMDL Support: Conduct comprehensive assessment of watershed non-point source pollution (e.g., agricultural runoff, urban surface runoff), identify key pollution sources and pollution load distribution, and provide data support for Total Maximum Daily Load (TMDL) formulation and implementation. It can simulate the migration and transformation of non-point source pollutants in the watershed, helping to formulate targeted pollution control plans.

  • Pipe Network Infiltration & RDII Simulation: Conduct in-depth research and simulation analysis on groundwater inflow and infiltration in urban pipe network systems, as well as rain-dependent inflow and infiltration (RDII). It can accurately calculate the infiltration volume and its impact on pipe network flow and water quality, helping to optimize pipe network maintenance plans and improve the accuracy of runoff simulation.

Introduction

Avaris CS 4.0.2

This chapter presents the conceptual framework that Avaris CS uses to model urban drainage systems and watershed water management. It outlines the principal physical elements, hydraulic structures, and process modules that constitute an integrated hydrological-hydraulic-water quality simulation model in Avaris CS.

Avaris CS conceptualizes an urban watershed and drainage system as an interconnected network of water, sediment, and pollutant flows passing through five major environmental compartments:

1.Atmosphere Compartment: Represents the atmospheric processes generating precipitation and depositing surface pollutants. In Avaris CS, rainfall inputs are defined using Rain Gage objects, while climatic parameters (temperature, evaporation, wind speed, snowmelt, and areal depletion) are managed via the Climatology system.

2.Land Surface Compartment: Represented by Subcatchment objects. It receives precipitation from the Atmosphere compartment and models surface hydrology, including infiltration, depression storage, surface runoff generation, Low Impact Development (LID) controls, and pollutant buildup/washoff.

3.Groundwater Compartment: Models the unsaturated and saturated subsurface soil layers beneath subcatchments using Aquifer parameters. It receives infiltrated water from the Land Surface compartment and exchanges interflow with the 1D pipe network.

4.Transport Compartment (1D Pipe Network): Consists of a 1D network of conveyance elements (Conduits, Pumps, Regulators) and junction/storage nodes (Junctions, Outfalls, Storages, Dividers). It transports surface runoff, sanitary dry weather flow (DWF), groundwater interflow, and rainfall-dependent inflow/infiltration (RDII) to downstream outfalls or treatment facilities.

5.2D Surface Flood Compartment (2D Surface Domain): Represents the overland surface domain for flood risk and inundation modeling. Utilizing unstructured/structured computational Meshes generated from Digital Elevation Models (DEM), this compartment seamlessly couples with the 1D transport network to simulate surface waterlogging, flood wave propagation, depth, and inundation duration.

Visual Objects

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Visual objects (also referred to as physical objects) represent the spatial and topological elements of the study area that are rendered directly in the Map View and managed within the Layer View of Avaris CS.

Rain Gages

Rain Gages supply precipitation data to one or more subcatchments within the study area. Precipitation data can be provided as a user-defined time series or loaded from external rain files.

The primary input properties of a Rain Gage in Avaris CS include:

  • Rainfall Data Type: Specifies how rainfall data is reported (e.g., Intensity, Volume, or Cumulative Volume).
  • Time Interval: Recording time step of the rainfall input (e.g., 5-minute, 1-hour).
  • Snow Catch Factor: Calibration multiplier for correction of snowfall catchment losses.
  • Time Series / File Source: Associated internal Time Series or external data file path.

Subcatchments

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Subcatchments are land hydrologic units whose topography and land-use characteristics route surface runoff to a single discharge outlet (either a drainage node or another subcatchment). Subcatchments are divided into pervious and impervious land subareas:

  • Impervious Subarea: Water cannot infiltrate through this surface. It is further divided into a portion with depression storage and a portion without (Zero-Imperv).
  • Pervious Subarea: Infiltration occurs into the upper soil zone based on selected infiltration models.

Avaris CS supports five infiltration models for pervious subareas:

  • Classic Horton infiltration
  • Modified Horton infiltration
  • Green-Ampt infiltration
  • Modified Green-Ampt infiltration
  • SCS Curve Number infiltration

Runoff can be internally routed between subareas via Subarea Routing options (Impervious, Pervious, or Outlet). To simulate Low Impact Development (LID) controls, users can assign predefined LID facilities (e.g., rain gardens, permeable pavements). Water quality buildup and wash-off processes are assigned to subcatchments via Land Use fractions.

Key input parameters for subcatchments include:

  • assigned rain gage
  • outlet node or subcatchment
  • total area
  • percent imperviousness area
  • average slope
  • characteristic width of overland flow
  • Manning's roughness (n) for overland flow on both pervious and impervious areas
  • depression storage in both pervious and impervious areas
  • percent of impervious area with no depression storage

Junction Nodes

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Junctions represent physical confluences, sewer manholes, pipe fittings, or channel intersections in the transport network. Inflows to junctions can originate from surface runoff, groundwater interflow, sanitary dry weather flow (DWF), direct external inflow hydrographs, or rainfall-dependent infiltration and inflow (RDII). Under surcharged conditions, water depth at a junction can rise above the crown of connecting conduits. If pressurized water exceeds the ground surface elevation, Avaris CS can either allow water to pond above the junction (if Allow Ponding is enabled) and drain back when network capacity frees up, or treat flooded water as lost from the system.

Inflow components at junctions include:

  • invert (channel or manhole bottom) elevation
  • height to ground surface
  • ponded surface area when flooded (optional)
  • external inflow data (optional)

Outfall Nodes

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Outfalls are terminal boundary nodes of the drainage system that discharge water out of the model domain. Under Dynamic Wave routing, outfalls provide downstream hydraulic boundary conditions. Under Kinematic Wave or Steady Flow routing, outfalls behave similarly to junctions.

Boundary condition stages at an outfall can be assigned using one of four stage relationships:

  • the critical or normal flow depth in the connecting conduit
  • a fixed stage elevation
  • a tidal stage described in a table of tide height versus hour of the day
  • a user-defined time series of stage versus time

Key input parameters for outfalls include:

  • invert elevation
  • boundary condition type and stage description
  • presence of a flap gate to prevent backflow through the outfall

Flow Divider Nodes

Flow Dividers are specialized nodes that split and divert incoming flow to a secondary discharge conduit according to user-defined diversion rules. Flow dividers are active during Steady Flow and Kinematic Wave routing, and act as standard junctions under Dynamic Wave routing. A flow divider can connect to a maximum of two discharge links.

The four types of flow diversion methods are:

1.Cutoff Divider: Diverts all inflow exceeding a specified cutoff flow threshold \( Q_{\mathit{cutoff}} \).

2.Overflow Divider: Diverts all inflow that exceeds the maximum hydraulic capacity of the non-diverted conduit.

3.Tabular Divider: Uses a lookup table specifying diverted flow as a function of total inflow.

4.Weir Divider: Computes diverted flow using an equivalent weir equation:

$$Q_{\mathit{div}}=C_w(fH_w)^{1.5}$$

where \( Q_{\mathit{div}} \) is the diverted flow rate, \( C_{\mathit{w}} \) is the weir coefficient, \( H_{\mathit{w}} \) is the weir height, and \( f \) is a scaling factor defined by:

$$f = \frac{Q_{\mathit{in}} - Q_{\mathit{min}}}{Q_{\mathit{max}} - Q_{\mathit{min}}}$$

where \( Q_{\mathit{in}} \) is total inflow, \( Q_{\mathit{min}} \) is the threshold flow where diversion begins, and \(Q_{\mathit{max}} = C_w H_w^{1.5}\).

Key input parameters for flow dividers include:

  • junction parameters (see above)
  • name of the link receiving the diverted flow
  • method used for computing the amount of diverted flow

Storage

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Storage are specialized nodes providing volumetric water storage capacity, representing structures ranging from catch basins and manholes to detention basins, retention ponds, and natural lakes.

The volume-depth relationship of a storage unit is defined either by a functional shape equation or a Storage Curve table relating surface area to water height. In addition to routing surface inflow and network outflow, storage units can model volumetric losses via surface evaporation and soil seepage/exfiltration.

Key input parameters for storage units include:

  • invert (bottom) elevation
  • maximum depth
  • depth-surface area data
  • evaporation potential
  • seepage parameters (optional)
  • external inflow data (optional)

Conduits

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Conduits are pipelines or open channels that move water between two nodes in the conveyance system. Avaris CS supports a comprehensive set of cross-sectional geometries:

  • Open Channels: Rectangular, Trapezoidal, Triangular, Parabolic, or Irregular cross-sections defined via Transect profile survey data.
  • Closed Conduits/Pipes: Circular, Rectangular Closed, Elliptical (Horizontal/Vertical), Arch, Filled Circular, Basket-handle, Egg, Horseshoe, Gothic, Catenary, and Custom Closed Shapes defined via Shape Curves.

Flow rate through standard gravity conduits is calculated using Manning's equation:

$$Q = \frac{1.49}{n} A R^{2/3} S^{1/2} \quad \textit{(US Customary Units)} \quad \textit{or} \quad Q = \frac{1.0}{n} A R^{2/3} S^{1/2} \quad \textit{(SI Units)}$$

where \( n \) is Manning's roughness coefficient, \( A \) is flow cross-sectional area, \( R \) is hydraulic radius, and \( S \) is conduit slope or friction slope.

Pumps

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Pumps are link elements used to raise water to higher hydraulic heads or boost flow through pressurized networks. The operational performance of a pump is governed by its assigned Pump Curve. Avaris CS supports five distinct pump curve types:

Pump Curve Types

Additionally, an Ideal Pump can be defined (using * as its pump curve ID). An ideal pump instantly transfers all incoming flow from its inlet node to its discharge node regardless of head differences.

Pump operation (startup, shutoff, speed modulation) can be dynamically regulated using startup/shutoff water depths or rule-based Control Rules.

Key input parameters for pumps include:

  • names of its inlet and outlet nodes
  • name of its pump curve (or * for an Ideal pump)
  • initial on/off status
  • startup and shutoff depths (optional)

Flow Regulators

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Flow Regulators are link elements used to control, throttle, or divert flow within the drainage network. Avaris CS models three main categories of flow regulators:
Orifices, Weirs, and Outlets.

Orifices

Orifices represent submerged or partially submerged openings in manhole walls, storage structures, or control gates. Under fully submerged conditions, discharge \( Q \) is computed as:

$$Q = CA\sqrt{2gh}$$

where \( C \) is the discharge coefficient, \( A \) is the orifice opening area, \( g \) is acceleration of gravity, and \( h \) is the effective head difference across the orifice. Under partially submerged conditions, flow transitions to an equivalent weir formula. Dynamic gate opening/closing operations can be simulated by modulating orifice height via Control Rules.

The principal input parameters for an orifice include:

  • names of its inlet and outlet nodes
  • configuration (bottom or side)
  • shape (circular or rectangular)
  • height or elevation above the inlet node invert
  • discharge coefficient
  • time to open or close (optional)

Weirs represent overflow structures placed across channels, along side embankments, or at storage spillways. Avaris CS supports five weir formulations:

Outlets

Outlets are custom head-discharge control devices typically installed at storage unit outlets. Outflows are governed by user-defined Rating Curves specifying discharge flow rate \( Q \) as a function of either depth above the opening or head difference Δ\( h \).

The principal input parameters for an outlet include:

  • names of its inlet and outlet nodes
  • height or elevation above the inlet node invert
  • function or table containing its head (or depth) discharge relationship

Non-Visual Objects

Avaris CS 4.0.2

In the Avaris CS integrated dynamic hydrological, hydraulic, and water quality modeling system, in addition to visual physical entities that can be spatially located, topologically drawn, and geometrically edited directly on the Map View (such as Subcatchments, Conduits, Junctions, and Outfalls), the software relies extensively on a class of data structures without direct spatial map representation—Non-Visual Objects. Non-visual objects are utilized to parameterize complex internal watershed processes, hydro-meteorological environmental drivers, subsurface groundwater dynamics, rainfall-dependent infiltration and inflow (RDII) response mechanisms, automated real-time control (RTC) logic for hydraulic structures, pollutant buildup and wash-off dynamics, as well as various non-linear hydraulic rating relationships. These objects establish the physical equation constraints and boundary conditions operating in the background of the computational engine.

Avaris CS provides a centralized management and interactive editing interface for all non-visual objects via the Parameter View located on the left panel of the main window. This structural design, which decouples spatial physical topology from non-spatial physical mechanisms, maintains map view clarity while providing maximum parameter reusability and mechanistic depth. By binding non-visual objects to specific visual entities, Avaris CS accurately reproduces the dynamic hydraulic and water quality processes of complex urban drainage networks and natural water bodies under dynamic storm events and continuous long-term climatic forcing.

Climatology

The Climatology module is a fundamental non-visual object responsible for driving the watershed water balance and surface thermal energy balance. Climatological data not only dictate the magnitude and temporal distribution of surface runoff generation during storm events, but also play a governing role during continuous long-term hydrologic simulations. During dry inter-event periods, surface evaporation and evapotranspiration represent the sole physical mechanisms for recovering surface Depression Storage and soil Infiltration Capacity. In cold climates, air temperature and wind speed directly control solid precipitation accumulation, sublimation, and spring snowmelt hydrograph peaks. Omitting the dynamic effects of evaporation and air temperature can lead to significant overestimation of surface runoff volumes and peak discharges during consecutive rainfall events.

The Climatology Editor in Avaris CS offers a comprehensive and flexible interface for configuring climatic boundaries. Engineers can configure seven core tabs via the Parameter View: Temperature, Evaporation, Wind Speed, Snowmelt, Areal Depletion, and Climate Adjustments. To support climate change impact assessments and extreme weather resilience planning, Avaris CS incorporates Climate Adjustments. This feature allows monthly multiplicative multipliers or additive adjustments to be applied to air temperature, evaporation rates, rainfall intensity, and soil hydraulic conductivity without altering the original historical time series, providing a robust sensitivity analysis tool for green infrastructure planning and flood risk management.

Temperature

Air temperature data are primarily used to determine precipitation form (rain vs. snow), simulate snowpack melting, and estimate daily potential evaporation rates. If snowmelt processes are not simulated in single-event rainstorm models, temperature data are not required. Avaris CS supports the following temperature data sources:

  • Time Series: Uses a user-defined point time series, with intermediate values automatically interpolated by the software. Units are in degrees Fahrenheit (°F) for US units and degrees Celsius (°C) for Metric units.
  • Climate File: Reads an external climate file containing daily minimum and maximum temperatures. Avaris CS fits a sinusoidal curve through these values based on the day of the year to derive hourly air temperatures. The climate file can also directly supply daily evaporation and wind speed data.

Evaporation

Evaporation can occur from standing water on subcatchment surfaces, subsurface water in aquifers, open channels, and storage units. Input evaporation rates represent potential evapotranspiration, whereas actual evaporation depends on moisture availability. Avaris CS supports the following evaporation rate specifications:

  • Constant Value: A single constant daily evaporation rate used throughout the simulation period (in/day or mm/day).
  • Monthly Average: A set of 12 monthly average daily evaporation rates.
  • Time Series: A user-defined time series specifying dynamic hourly or daily evaporation rates.
  • Temperatures: Daily evaporation rates computed automatically from daily minimum and maximum temperatures read from an external climate file.
  • Climate File: Daily pan evaporation values read directly from an external climate file. Monthly pan coefficients must be supplied to convert pan data to free water-surface values. An option is also available to restrict evaporation strictly to dry periods without precipitation.

Wind Speed

Wind speed is an optional climatic variable used exclusively in snowmelt energy-balance calculations. During warming periods, wind speed governs sensible and latent heat flux exchanges between the atmosphere and snowpack surfaces. Avaris CS supports the following wind speed data:

  • Monthly Average: A set of 12 monthly average wind speeds (mph or km/h).
  • Climate File: Daily wind speed values read directly from the same external climate file used for daily temperatures.

Snowmelt Parameters

Snowmelt parameters are global climatic variables applied across the entire study area to govern snowfall formation and melt dynamics. Core parameters include:

  • Rain/Snow Temperature: The threshold air temperature below which precipitation falls as snow.
  • Heat Exchange Properties: Snow surface energy-exchange coefficients governing heat absorption and melt rates during melting periods.
  • Elevation, Latitude, and Longitude Correction: Adjustments applied to atmospheric pressure and air temperature based on the study area's geographic position and mean elevation.

Areal Depletion

Areal depletion refers to the non-uniform melting behavior of snowpacks over a subcatchment due to topographic relief, slope aspect, and localized solar radiation variation. This process is represented by an Areal Depletion Curve (ADC), which plots the fraction of total area remaining snow-covered against the snow depth ratio (the ratio of actual snow depth to the depth required for 100% area coverage).

Areal depletion curve for a natural area

Figure 3-3 illustrates a typical Areal Depletion Curve for a natural watershed. The horizontal axis represents the fraction of snow-covered area, while the vertical axis represents the relative snow depth ratio. The curve exhibits pronounced non-linear behavior: under deep snowpacks (snow depth ratio near 1.0), the entire area maintains 100% snow coverage. As melting progresses and the snow depth ratio decreases, snow on south-facing slopes and ridge tops melts first, causing snow-covered area to decay rapidly along an S-shaped curve. In late melt stages, residual snow retreats into sheltered valleys and shaded hollows, where low coverage ratios sustain residual snow depth. Avaris CS utilizes this curve to dynamically adjust solar radiation absorption area and surface albedo, ensuring that the recession limb of the snowmelt hydrograph matches physical observations. Independent depletion curves can be defined for pervious and impervious subareas.

Climate Adjustments

Climate Adjustments allow periodic monthly adjustment factors (multipliers or additions) to be applied to air temperature, evaporation rates, and rainfall intensity. Furthermore, a set of monthly adjustment factors can be applied to the soil saturated hydraulic conductivity of all pervious land surfaces (including LID controls) to reflect seasonal ground freezing or temperature-dependent water viscosity changes. Subcatchments can override global infiltration adjustments by assigning dedicated monthly time patterns.

Snow Packs

Snow Pack objects contain parameters that characterize the accumulation, spatial redistribution, sublimation, liquid water detention, and melting of snow over a watershed. In cold climates or high-altitude basins, solid snowfall forms temporary ground storage and does not immediately generate surface runoff. Runoff occurs only when air temperatures exceed the melting threshold or during rain-on-snow events, releasing stored liquid water into the drainage system. Without accurate representation of snowpack dynamics, hydrologic models cannot reliably predict spring snowmelt peak flows or contaminant flush timing.

Avaris CS discretizes each subcatchment into three distinct subareas for snowpack accounting:

  • Plowable Snow Pack Area: Covers a user-defined fraction of the total impervious area (e.g., streets, parking lots) where snow plowing, removal, and dumping operations take place.
  • Impervious Snow Pack Area: Covers the remaining non-plowable impervious area of a subcatchment (e.g., building rooftops).
  • Pervious Snow Pack Area: Encompasses the entire pervious surface area of a subcatchment (e.g., lawns, open space).

Each of these three subareas is characterized by the following parameters:

  • Minimum and Maximum Snow Melt Coefficients
  • Minimum Air Temperature for Snow Melt
  • Snow Depth for 100% Areal Coverage
  • Initial Snow Depth
  • Initial and Maximum Free Water Content

In addition, a set of Snow Removal Parameters can be assigned to the Plowable area. These include the threshold snow depth that triggers plowing operations, and the fractions of snow exported out of the basin, moved to pervious subareas, moved to non-plowable impervious subareas, converted to immediate melt, or transferred to another subcatchment. A single Snow Pack object can be assigned to multiple subcatchments, with Avaris CS maintaining independent physical snowpack state variables for each subcatchment in the background.

Aquifers

Aquifer objects represent subsurface groundwater zones located beneath subcatchments. They model the vertical recharge of water infiltrating from overlying soil zones, as well as the dynamic exchange between groundwater and the drainage network—either as groundwater infiltration (Baseflow) into conduits or exfiltration from conveyance channels into the aquifer, governed by local hydraulic gradients. Aquifers are essential for long-term continuous simulations, baseflow recession curve calibration, and groundwater infiltration (GWI) analysis.

In the Avaris CS conceptual model, an aquifer is vertically discretized into an Unsaturated Zone and a Saturated Zone. Key hydraulic parameters include:

  • Soil Porosity and Field Capacity
  • Saturated Hydraulic Conductivity
  • Evapotranspiration Depth and evapotranspiration loss coefficients

Unit Hydrographs

Unit Hydrograph (UH) objects in Avaris CS estimate Rainfall-Dependent Infiltration and Inflow (RDII) entering sanitary or combined sewer systems. Pollutant Buildup

Buildup accumulation is normalized per unit subcatchment area (Mass/Area) or per unit curb length (Mass/Curb Length). Buildup is a function of antecedent dry-weather days and follows one of these formulations:

Power Function: Models buildup accumulation as an escalating power function of antecedent dry days, representing accelerated initial deposition that levels off abruptly once a specified maximum storage capacity ceiling is reached:

$$B = \min(C_1, C_2 \cdot t^{C_3})$$

where \( C_{\mathit{1}} \) = maximum buildup capacity (mass per unit area or curb length), \( C_{\mathit{2}} \) = buildup rate constant, \( C_{\mathit{3}} \) = time exponent, and \( t \) = antecedent dry-weather duration (days).

Exponential Function: Characterizes buildup accumulation using a decaying growth rate, simulating a physical process where initial rapid mass deposition gradually slows as surface storage nears its ultimate asymptotic limit due to natural losses like wind action or self-limiting deposition:

$$B = C_1(1 - e^{-C_2 \cdot t})$$

where \( C_{\mathit{1}} \) = maximum asymptotic buildup capacity (mass per unit area or curb length), \( C_{\mathit{2}} \) = first-order daily accumulation rate constant (1/days), and \( t \) = antecedent dry time (days).

Saturation Function: Assumes a quasi-linear buildup rate during early dry weather periods that smoothly transitions into a non-linear decay curve bounded by a maximum capacity limit. This is ideal for modeling urban surfaces where accumulation rates steadily diminish over extended dry intervals:

$$B = \frac{C_1 \cdot t}{C_2 + t}$$

where \( C_{\mathit{1}} \) = maximum storage capacity limit (mass per unit area or curb length), \( C_{\mathit{2}} \) = half-saturation time constant (days required to reach 50% of maximum buildup), and \( t \) = antecedent dry duration (days).

External Time Series: Bypasses analytical formulation by allowing users to supply an external time series defining a variable daily accumulation rate (mass per unit area or curb length per day). A scaling factor multiplier and a maximum buildup limit can be optionally specified.

Pollutant Washoff

Pollutant washoff from a given land use category occurs during wet weather rain events and describes the mobilization and transport of accumulated surface mass into the stormwater conveyance system. Washoff can be specified using one of the following formulations:

Exponential Washoff: Assumes that the rate of pollutant mass mobilization is proportional to both the remaining mass of surface buildup and the kinetic energy of runoff (represented by unit-area runoff rate). As runoff washes away surface mass, the available buildup continuously depletes, causing the wash-off rate to decay exponentially over time:

$$W = C_1 \cdot q^{C_2} \cdot B$$

where \( W \) = washoff mass load rate ($(\textit{mass}/hr)$), \( C_{\mathit{1}} \) = washoff mobilization coefficient, \( C_{\mathit{2}} \) = runoff rate exponent, \( q \) = unit-area runoff rate (in/hr or mm/hr), and \( B \) = current total remaining surface buildup mass.

Rating Curve Washoff: Calculates mass mobilization purely as a function of the total volumetric discharge rate from the subcatchment, independent of the mass or history of dry-weather buildup stored on the land surface. This method is suited for catchments where pollutant supply is effectively unlimited or continuously replenished:

$$W = C_1 \cdot Q^{C_2}$$

where \( W \) = washoff mass emission rate ($(\textit{mass}/sec)$), \( C_{\mathit{1}} \) = rating washoff coefficient, \( C_{\mathit{2}} \) = discharge exponent, and \( Q \) = total subcatchment runoff discharge rate in active model units.

Event Mean Concentration (EMC): A simplified linear case of Rating Curve Washoff (where exponent \( C_{\mathit{2}} \)=1.0). It assumes that stormwater runoff maintains a constant, uniform pollutant concentration throughout the rain event, completely bypassing dry-weather accumulation dynamics:

$$W = C_1 \cdot Q$$

where \( C_{\mathit{1}} \) = specified constant runoff concentration (mass per liter, e.g., mg/L or ug/L) and \( Q \) = instantaneous runoff discharge rate. Under the EMC option, surface buildup modeling is bypassed entirely.

Low Impact Development (LID) Controls

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Low Impact Development (LID) Controls represent green infrastructure practices designed to capture, detain, infiltrate, and evapotranspire surface runoff at the source. Unlike traditional subcatchment surface runoff modeling where rainfall immediately transforms into overland flow, LID controls incorporate multi-layered physical media (such as surface storage ponds, engineered soil mixtures, aggregate storage layers, and perforated underdrain pipes) to detain and purify stormwater. Avaris CS explicitly models vertical water movement, soil moisture redistribution, and storage dynamics across each layer, allowing engineers to quantitatively evaluate green infrastructure performance in reducing runoff volume, dampening peak flow rates, and mitigating nonpoint source pollution loadings.

Avaris CS explicitly models eight generic types of LID controls:

Bio-retention Cells: Depressions containing vegetation grown in an engineered soil layer placed above a gravel storage bed (with optional underdrains). They provide storage, infiltration, and evapotranspiration.

Rain Gardens: A variation of bio-retention cells consisting solely of an engineered soil layer without a gravel storage bed beneath it.

Green Roofs: Bio-retention variations installed on rooftops, featuring a light growth medium layer atop a specialized drainage mat.

Infiltration Trenches: Gravel-filled ditches that intercept runoff from upslope impervious areas, providing temporary storage and enhancing native soil infiltration.

Permeable Pavement Systems: Excavated areas filled with aggregate stone and paved over with porous concrete/asphalt or permeable block pavers.

Rain Barrels / Cisterns: On-site storage containers that collect roof runoff during storm events for later release or dry-weather non-potable re-use.

Rooftop Disconnection: Redirects roof downspouts to discharge onto pervious landscaped lawns rather than directly into storm drains.

Vegetative Swales: Shallow vegetated channels that slow conveyance velocities, promoting infiltration into native subsoil.


When incorporating LID controls into a watershed model, Avaris CS supports two distinct spatial placement approaches:

  • Placing controls within an existing subcatchment: One or more LID units are embedded into an existing subcatchment, displacing an equivalent amount of non-LID surface area. Under this approach, LIDs act in parallel (i.e., they treat runoff generated from the non-LID portion of the subcatchment, but outflows from one LID cannot be routed sequentially into another LID within the same subcatchment).
  • Creating a dedicated LID subcatchment: A new subcatchment is created and devoted 100% to a single LID practice.

Adjustment of subcatchment parameters after LID placement

Figure 3-6 illustrates the spatial reallocation of subcatchment surface subareas before and after introducing an LID control (such as permeable pavement). When an LID is placed within an existing subcatchment, it occupies a physical portion of the original catchment area, thereby shrinking the remaining non-LID surface area. Consequently, the subcatchment's overall Percent Imperviousness (% Imperv) and Characteristic Width (Width) properties must be mathematically re-calibrated for the remaining non-LID area to avoid double-counting or miscalculating overland flow path lengths.

For example, consider a 10-acre subcatchment that is initially 40% impervious (4 acres impervious, 6 acres pervious). Suppose 75% of that impervious area (3 acres) is converted into a permeable pavement LID control. The remaining non-LID area of the subcatchment becomes 7 acres (1 acre impervious, 6 acres pervious). To ensure that Avaris CS correctly computes overland flow over the non-LID portion, the subcatchment's adjusted Percent Imperviousness (\( I_{\mathit{new}} \)) must be recalculated as the remaining impervious area divided by the total remaining non-LID area:

$$\mathit{Adjusted}\%\mathit{Imperv} = \frac{(1 - 0.75) \times 40\%}{100\% - (0.75 \times 40\%)} \equiv \frac{10\%}{70\%} = \mathbf{14.3\%}$$

Properly applying this parameter adjustment ensures that the runoff hydrograph generated by the non-LID subarea remains hydraulically accurate, while the permeable pavement LID independently processes its dedicated fraction of captured runoff.

Bio-retention cells, infiltration trenches, and permeable pavement systems can incorporate optional underdrain systems in their aggregate storage beds to convey excess filtered runoff away from the site and prevent surface flooding. They can also feature impermeable liners to prevent infiltration into native soil where groundwater protection is required. Furthermore, infiltration trenches and permeable pavements can simulate clogging over time by specifying a progressive reduction in hydraulic conductivity. For LID units equipped with underdrains, specific pollutant removal percentages can be assigned to the drain discharge. Overall, LIDs reduce both runoff discharge volume and total pollutant mass loading delivered to receiving water bodies.

Surface Runoff

Avaris CS 4.0.2

The conceptual framework for surface runoff generation in Avaris CS treats each subcatchment surface as a nonlinear reservoir. Under this formulation, the subcatchment surface acts as a physical storage container that receives water inputs from precipitation and upstream surface runon. Water exits the reservoir through three primary pathways: evaporation, soil infiltration, and surface runoff.

The total capacity of this nonlinear reservoir corresponds to the subcatchment's maximum depression storage (\( d_{\mathit{s}} \)), which represents the combined surface storage provided by micro-topographic depression ponding, surface wetting, and vegetative canopy interception. Surface runoff per unit area initiates only when the accumulated water depth (\( d \)) over the subcatchment strictly exceeds the maximum depression storage depth (\( d_{\mathit{s}} \)). When \( d \)>\( d_{\mathit{s}} \), the excess ponded depth (\( d \)−\( d_{\mathit{s}} \)) drives surface sheet flow, which is computed using Manning's equation for overland flow. Throughout the simulation, Avaris CS continuously updates the water depth \( d \) at each time step by numerically solving the non-linear water balance differential equation for each subcatchment subarea.

Conceptual view of surface runoff

Figure 3-7 illustrates the conceptual nonlinear reservoir model used by Avaris CS for surface runoff calculations. The diagram details the vertical water depth \( d \) and depression storage threshold \( d_{\mathit{s}} \) across the subcatchment surface. Inflow fluxes consist of direct atmospheric precipitation and upstream surface runon. Outflow fluxes include surface evaporation into the atmosphere, infiltration down into the soil column, and overland runoff discharging horizontally to receiving outlets. Runoff generation is governed by a threshold mechanism: when total depth \( d \) is less than or equal to \( d_{\mathit{s}} \) , water is retained within depression storage and no surface runoff occurs; when depth \( d \) exceeds \( d_{\mathit{s}} \) , the depth excess (\( d \)−\( d_{\mathit{s}} \)) is converted into surface runoff discharge governed by surface roughness and slope.

Infiltration

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Infiltration is the physical process by which rainfall penetrates the ground surface and enters the unsaturated soil zone of pervious land surfaces. In Avaris CS, infiltration governs the partition of net rainfall between surface runoff generation and subsurface moisture storage. The software offers five distinct computational methods for modeling soil infiltration:

  • Horton's Method: Based on empirical observations showing that soil infiltration capacity decreases exponentially from an initial maximum rate toward a minimum saturated rate over the course of a prolonged rainfall event. Input parameters include the maximum infiltration rate, minimum infiltration rate, a decay coefficient describing the exponential reduction rate over time, and a drying time required for a saturated soil to fully drain.
  • Modified Horton Method: A modified formulation of the classical Horton method that utilizes cumulative infiltration in excess of the minimum rate as its governing state variable (rather than elapsed time). This prevents the unrealistic depletion of infiltration capacity during initial low-intensity rainfall periods, providing improved physical estimates for intermittent rainfall events.
  • Green-Ampt Method: A physically based infiltration model that assumes the existence of a sharp wetting front separating an upper saturated soil layer from a lower unsaturated soil layer with initial moisture content. Input parameters include the initial soil moisture deficit, soil saturated hydraulic conductivity, and wetting front suction head. The recovery of soil moisture deficit during dry weather periods is empirically linked to soil hydraulic conductivity.
  • Modified Green-Ampt Method: Modifies the standard Green-Ampt formulation by preventing the depletion of soil moisture deficit in the upper soil layer during initial periods of low rainfall intensity that are less than the soil's saturated hydraulic conductivity. This modification yields realistic infiltration responses during storm events characterized by long, low-intensity initial rainfall tails.
  • Curve Number Method: An empirical approach adapted from the NRCS (SCS) Curve Number methodology. It assumes that total soil infiltration capacity is determined from a tabulated Curve Number (CN) representing land use, hydrologic soil group, and antecedent moisture conditions. During a storm event, infiltration capacity is depleted as a function of cumulative rainfall and remaining capacity. Input parameters include the CN value and the soil drying time.

In addition, Avaris CS allows the soil infiltration recovery rate to be adjusted on a monthly basis using an optional Infiltration Pattern specified in Climatology settings, accounting for seasonal variations in groundwater levels, soil temperature, and evapotranspiration rates.

Groundwater

Avaris CS 4.0.2

The Groundwater module in Avaris CS simulates vertical water movement within subsurface soil layers and models two-way flow exchanges between shallow saturated aquifers and drainage network nodes or open channels. In unlined channels or areas with high water tables, shallow groundwater interactions significantly influence dry-weather baseflow, pipeline infiltration, and post-storm hydrograph recession curves.

Avaris CS conceptualizes groundwater using a vertical two-zone model consisting of an upper Unsaturated Zone and a lower Saturated Zone. The unsaturated upper zone has a variable moisture content (θ), whereas the saturated lower zone is fully saturated with a fixed moisture content equal to the soil porosity (ϕ).

Two-zone groundwater model

Figure 3-8 presents a schematic of the two-zone groundwater model implemented in Avaris CS. The subsurface column is partitioned into an Upper Unsaturated Zone (depth \( d_{\mathit{u}} \), ground elevation \( E_{\mathit{g}} \)) and a Lower Saturated Zone (depth \( d_{\mathit{L}} \), bed floor elevation \( E_{\mathit{B}} \)). Volumetric fluxes per unit area include: \( f_{\mathit{I}} \) (infiltration from the surface), \( f_{\mathit{E}} \) (upper zone evapotranspiration), \( f_{\mathit{U}} \) (percolation from unsaturated to saturated zone driven by upper moisture content θ), \( f_{\mathit{EG}} \) (lower zone evapotranspiration), \( f_{\mathit{L}} \) (deep percolation seepage to lower unmodeled aquifers), and \( f_{\mathit{G}} \) (lateral groundwater interflow to receiving channel or node). Avaris CS computes a dynamic water mass balance across both zones at each time step to update the water table elevation and unsaturated soil moisture content.

Lateral groundwater exchange flow (\( f_{\mathit{G}} \)) between the lower saturated zone and a receiving drainage node depends on the water table elevation, node invert elevation, and water depth inside the receiving node or open channel, enabling two-way simulation of groundwater infiltration and surface water exfiltration.

Snowmelt

Avaris CS 4.0.2

The Snowmelt routine in Avaris CS is integrated directly into the surface runoff modeling engine. It continuously tracks snowpack accumulation, snow redistribution via plowing operations and areal depletion, and snowmelt via thermal heat budget calculations. Meltwater discharging from the snowpack is treated as an additional, effective rainfall input applied directly onto the subcatchment surface.

At each runoff computational time step, Avaris CS executes the following sequential calculations:

1.Updates ambient air temperature and melt coefficients according to the current calendar date.

2.Converts any precipitation falling when air temperature is below the rain/snow threshold into solid snow accumulation within the snowpack.

3.Redistributes excess snow depth across the plowable snowpack area based on user-defined snow removal parameters (e.g., plowing, dumping, or inter-subcatchment transfer).

4.Adjusts the actual snow-covered area fraction across pervious and impervious subareas using the specified Areal Depletion Curves (ADC).

5.Computes the snowmelt rate:
a.Uses an energy-balance heat budget equation during rainfall periods, where melt rate escalates with increasing air temperature, wind speed, and rain intensity.
b.Uses a degree-day equation during dry periods, where melt rate is proportional to the difference between air temperature and the pack's base melt temperature.

6.Updates snowpack temperature and cold content: if air temperature is below freezing, pack temperature drops; if melting occurs, melt heat increases pack temperature up to the freezing point (32\(^\circ\text{F}\) or 0\(^\circ\text{C}\)).

7.Deducts meltwater required to satisfy the snowpack's free water holding capacity. Any excess liquid meltwater is immediately released onto the subcatchment surface as effective rainfall.

Flow Routing

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Flow routing through conveyance links (Conduits) in Avaris CS is governed by the 1D Saint-Venant equations for gradually varied, unsteady open channel flow (conservation of mass and momentum). Avaris CS provides three distinct levels of routing sophistication:

Steady Flow Routing

Steady Flow Routing represents the simplest routing method by assuming uniform, steady flow conditions within each computational time step. It translates inflow hydrographs from the upstream end of a conduit to the downstream end without time delay or hydrograph attenuation. The normal flow Manning equation is used to relate flow discharge to water depth and area. Steady flow routing cannot account for channel storage, backwater effects, entrance/exit losses, flow reversal, or pressurized surcharging. It is restricted to dendritic (tree-like) pipe networks and is primarily suited for rapid, preliminary long-term continuous simulations.

Kinematic Wave Routing

Kinematic Wave Routing solves the 1D continuity equation along with a simplified momentum equation assuming that the energy slope equals the conduit slope. The maximum flow conveyed through a conduit is limited to its full normal flow capacity; any excess flow at the inlet node either ponds atop the node or is lost from the system. Kinematic wave routing allows flow rate and area to vary spatially and temporally along a conduit, capturing hydrograph attenuation and travel time delay. However, it cannot model backwater effects, local entrance/exit head losses, flow reversal, or pressurized pipe surcharging. It is restricted to dendritic layouts but maintains numerical stability under relatively large time steps (e.g., 1 to 5 minutes).

Dynamic Wave Routing

Dynamic Wave Routing solves the complete 1D Saint-Venant equations (continuity and momentum for links, volume continuity for nodes), producing the most theoretically rigorous results. It fully accounts for channel storage, backwater effects, entrance/exit losses, flow reversal, and pressurized surcharging. When a closed conduit becomes surcharged, flow can exceed normal full capacity under pressure heads. Dynamic wave routing couples water depth solutions at nodes with link discharge calculations, allowing it to be applied to any arbitrary network layout, including looped pipe networks, parallel channels, and multiple downstream diversions. It requires smaller computational time steps \((\textit{typically} \le 30\ \textit{seconds})\) to maintain numerical stability.

For pressurized circular force mains, Avaris CS allows hydraulic head loss to be computed using either the Hazen-Williams equation or the Darcy-Weisbach equation.

Ponding and Pressurization

Avaris CS 4.0.2

In standard flow routing, when inflow to a junction exceeds the conveyance capacity of downstream outlets, excess water overflows the junction rim and is removed from the system. Avaris CS offers an optional Allow Ponding feature where excess overflow volume is stored atop the junction in a virtual surface pond and subsequently re-introduced into the sewer system as downstream capacity becomes available.

Under Steady Flow and Kinematic Wave routing, ponded water is stored simply as a cumulative volume. Under Dynamic Wave Routing, which directly couples nodal water depth with link flow, ponded water is assumed to accumulate over a specified constant surface area assigned to the junction. Alternatively, surface overflow paths can be explicitly modeled using street channels, gutters, or surface swales connecting adjacent manholes.

In surcharged sewer networks under Dynamic Wave routing, hydraulic head at junction nodes can exceed ground rim elevations. Avaris CS allows users to specify an additional Surcharge Depth for junction nodes. This allows the manhole to pressurize up to the surcharge depth without overflowing. If both ponding and surcharge depth are enabled for a node, ponding takes precedence.

Water Quality Routing

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Water quality routing in Avaris CS models the transport, continuous mixing, and dynamic degradation of pollutants as stormwater and wastewater move through conveyance pipes, open channels, and storage structures. By coupling constituent mass transport directly with the dynamic hydraulic solution engine, the software accurately tracks temporal and spatial concentration variations throughout the entire drainage network.

Within individual conveyance links (such as conduits and open channels), water quality routing is modeled using the Continuously Stirred Tank Reactor (CSTR) complete-mixing framework. Under this formulation, any pollutant mass entering a pipe segment during a computational time step is assumed to mix rapidly and uniformly throughout the total volume of water stored within that segment. While ideal plug-flow models describe pure advective transport without dispersion, the CSTR complete-mixing approach provides exceptional numerical stability and computational efficiency during unsteady dynamic wave routing. When the travel time through a conduit segment is comparable to the routing time step, the CSTR model yields highly accurate approximations of actual longitudinal dispersion and advective mass transport.

Water quality routing within Storage Unit nodes (such as detention ponds, retention basins, and wet wells) follows the identical CSTR complete-mixing principle. As inflow hydrographs and pollutant mass loadings enter a storage facility, the constituent mass distributes uniformly across the active storage volume. The resulting concentration within the facility directly governs the water quality of both effluent discharges and surface overflows during that time step.

For non-storage conveyance nodes with zero storage volume (such as standard Junctions and Dividers), no pollutant mass accumulation or detention takes place. Instead, the constituent concentration exiting the node is calculated as the instantaneous, flow-weighted mixture concentration of all incoming flow streams, including tributary pipe discharges, direct time-series inflows, and sanitary dry weather flows.

When pollutants undergo physical, chemical, or biological degradation (such as biochemical oxygen demand breakdown, bacterial die-off, or chemical oxidation), Avaris CS incorporates a first-order decay reaction. For any constituent assigned a non-zero decay coefficient, the total mass retained within conduits and storage units is continuously reduced over each routing time step according to its specific decay rate constant and the current water volume.

LID Representation

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Low Impact Development (LID) Controls (such as bio-retention cells, rain gardens, green roofs, and permeable pavements) are conceptualized in Avaris CS as multi-layered, vertical physical models composed of distinct structural layers. To facilitate modular engineering design and parameter reusability, all physical layer properties are defined on a per-unit-area basis. This unit-area parameterization mechanism allows engineers to construct standardized LID design templates and seamlessly deploy them across multiple subcatchments of varying sizes within the study area, with the software automatically scaling total storage volumes and flow rates proportional to the actual footprint area assigned.

During dynamic simulation, the Avaris CS computational engine solves a continuous moisture balance differential equation across each structural layer at every time step. This formulation rigorously tracks vertical moisture flux movement between adjacent layers, layer moisture retention, evapotranspiration losses, underdrain discharge, and native subsoil infiltration.

Conceptual diagram of a bio-retention cell LID

Figure 3-9 illustrates the vertical layer structure and internal hydraulic flow pathways parameterized in Avaris CS for a Bio-Retention Cell LID unit. The unit consists of three primary vertical layers: an upper Surface Layer receiving direct precipitation and runon from surrounding non-LID subcatchment surfaces, an intermediate Soil Layer serving as engineered growth media, and a lower Storage Layer composed of aggregate stone. Inflow fluxes include atmospheric rainfall and surface runon. Outflow fluxes comprise surface overflow (occurring when ponded depth exceeds weir storage), evapotranspiration (ET) from surface ponding and soil moisture, vertical percolation through the root zone, perforated underdrain discharge, and exfiltration into native subsoil. This multi-layer coupling allows Avaris CS to evaluate the hydrograph attenuation, peak lag time, and volumetric reduction capabilities of green infrastructure under dynamic storm events.

The generic physical layers available in Avaris CS to construct LID control practices include:

  • Surface Layer: The ground or pavement surface that receives direct rainfall and upstream surface runon, retains excess water in surface depression storage, and generates surface overflow when the storage capacity is exceeded.
  • Pavement Layer: The porous concrete, permeable asphalt, or modular paver block layer used in permeable pavement systems, which governs the initial surface infiltration rate into the underlying matrix.
  • Soil Layer: The engineered soil mixture used in bio-retention cells and rain gardens to support vegetative growth, retain soil moisture, and filter urban pollutants.
  • Storage Layer: An aggregate bed of crushed stone or gravel providing high void storage in bio-retention cells, permeable pavements, and infiltration trenches; for rain barrels, this corresponds to the container storage volume itself.
  • Drain System: Perforated underdrain piping situated at the bottom of aggregate storage beds to convey filtered stormwater effluent away from the facility into receiving conveyance nodes.
  • Drainage Mat Layer: A specialized drainage mat or plate positioned between the growth media and waterproof membrane in green roof systems to convey percolated water off the roof deck.

Subcatchment Properties

Name: User-defined unique name for the project's subcatchment. Duplicate names are not allowed not only between subcatchments, but also between subcatchments and junctions. This is because the outlet of a subcatchment can be directed to either another subcatchment or a junction.

Curb Length: Total length of curbs in the subcatchment (ft or m). Used only when pollutant buildup is normalized to curb length.

Area: Area of the subcatchment , including any LID controls (ac or ha).

Width: Characteristic width of the overland flow path for sheet flow runoff (ft or m). An initial estimate of the characteristic width is given by the subcatchment area divided by the average maximum overland flow length. The maximum overland flow length is the length of the flow path from the furthest drainage point of the subcatchment before the flow becomes channelized. Maximum lengths from several different possible flow paths should be averaged. These paths should reflect slow, such as over pervious surfaces, more than rapid flow over pavement, for example. Adjustments should be made to the width parameter to produce good fits to measured runoff hydrographs.

Slope: Average percent slope of the subcatchment (%).

Imperv.: Percent of land area (excluding the area used for LID controls) which is impervious.

  • Impervious: runoff from pervious area flows to impervious area.
  • Pervious: runoff from impervious area flows to pervious area.

CN values vary from 0 to 100; a larger CN means more rainfall converts to surface runoff, while a smaller CN represents stronger infiltration capacity. Inertial Terms indicate how to handle the inertia terms in the Saint-Venant momentum equations:

  • KEEP – Always retain the full value of the inertia terms under all conditions.
  • DAMPEN – Reduce the inertia terms when flow approaches critical conditions, and ignore them when flow becomes supercritical.
  • IGNORE – Remove all inertia terms from the momentum equation, resulting in an essentially diffusion wave solution method.

Flow Regime Criteria determine when supercritical flow occurs in a conduit. The criteria include:

  • Water surface slope only (i.e., water surface slope > conduit slope)
  • Froude number only (i.e., Froude number > 1.0)
  • Both water surface slope and Froude number

    • The third option (checking both) is the recommended setting in current versions.

Simulation Files Settings

In large-scale urban drainage and watershed hydro-hydraulic modeling, as the catchment area expands and the pipe network topology grows increasingly complex, executing coupled hydro-hydraulic-water quality simulations for the entire system simultaneously requires significant computational resources and extended execution times. To enhance computational efficiency, enable distributed collaborative modeling, and streamline multi-scenario comparative analyses, Avaris CS incorporates a mature Interface Files mechanism. Interface files are utilized to store externally imposed time series inputs (such as precipitation or rainfall-dependent infiltration and inflow) or intermediate hydrographs and pollutographs generated during previous simulation runs (such as surface runoff hydrographs, outfall discharge hydrographs, and full-system hydraulic states).

By leveraging interface files, Avaris CS decouples the integrated "surface runoff generation – pipe network transport – water quality evolution" process into modular, independent sub-tasks. For instance, when evaluating multiple engineering alternatives for pipe capacity expansion or pump station operational rules, engineers can execute the surface hydrology calculations once and export the runoff results to a runoff interface file. In subsequent pipe network optimization runs, Avaris CS reads this interface file directly as nodal inflow inputs, bypassing redundant surface hydrological computations and reducing simulation run times by several orders of magnitude. Furthermore, interface files facilitate seamless model integration and boundary data sharing across different administrative sectors and sub-basins, providing crucial technical support for urban smart drainage digital twins and distributed real-time control (RTC) systems. In Avaris CS, users can flexibly specify the import and export paths for various interface files via the Files tab in Simulation Options. The different types of interface files that are currently available include:

  • rainfall interface file
  • runoff interface file
  • hot start file
  • RDII interface file
  • routing interface files

Rainfall and Runoff Files

Rainfall and runoff interface files are internal binary files created by Avaris CS to store and reuse intermediate computational data across consecutive simulation runs.

In large-scale watershed modeling involving multiple rain gages, reading external raw text precipitation files repeatedly generates substantial disk I/O overhead. The Rainfall Interface File automatically collates, time-aligns, and binary-compresses precipitation data from multiple separate rain gages into a single processed file. While Avaris CS automatically creates and deletes temporary rainfall interface files during standard runs, users can instruct the software to save the compiled rainfall interface file during the initial run and reuse it in subsequent iterations, significantly accelerating model initialization and execution. It is important to distinguish the binary rainfall interface file generated internally by Avaris CS from raw text rainfall data files provided by users.

The Runoff Interface File is used to save surface runoff hydrographs and pollutant wash-off mass loadings generated by subcatchments during a simulation run. When optimizing underground sewer pipe sizes, storage basin volumes, or green infrastructure sizing, surface subcatchment infiltration and runoff parameters typically remain unchanged. By saving and reusing the runoff interface file, Avaris CS bypasses surface hydrology and soil infiltration computations in subsequent runs, injecting stored runoff hydrographs and pollutographs directly into corresponding junction manholes to achieve rapid dynamic wave hydraulic and water quality solutions.

RDII Files

A Hot Start File is a binary snapshot generated by Avaris CS at the conclusion of a simulation run, capturing the complete instantaneous hydrologic, hydraulic, and water quality state of the study area.

When using the Dynamic Wave flow routing solver to solve 1D Saint-Venant equations for complex pipe networks or natural open channels, starting a simulation from a completely dry bed ("dry pipe" initial condition) while suddenly introducing intense storm inflows often causes severe numerical instabilities, artificial surcharge spikes, or model divergence. To eliminate initial numerical shock, standard municipal engineering practice involves pre-conditioning the network with a constant dry weather flow (DWF) for sewer networks or a steady baseflow for natural streams until steady-state hydraulic equilibrium is achieved. Saving this steady state as a hot start file enables subsequent storm simulations to initialize smoothly under stable hydraulic conditions, ensuring numerical convergence.

A hot start file created by Avaris CS preserves comprehensive state variables, including:

  • the depth of ponded surface water along with its constituent quality for each subcatchment
  • the mass of accumulated surface pollutants residing on each subcatchment
  • the soil infiltration status and moisture absorption dynamics across individual subcatchments
  • the snow accumulation, pack temperature, and melt conditions on each subcatchment
  • the vadose-zone moisture content, groundwater table elevation, and subsurface interflow for each subcatchment with an active groundwater zone
  • the hydraulic head/depth, lateral inflow rate, and pollutant concentrations at each system node
  • the discharge flow rate, water depth, operational regulator setting, and water quality within each conveyance link
💡 Note

The internal hydrological moisture states of Low Impact Development (LID) controls are not saved in hot start files

Beyond mitigating initial numerical shocks, hot start files facilitate sequential long-term continuous simulations. Multi-year continuous runs can be partitioned into manageable seasonal or annual segments, where the hot start file saved at the end of one segment serves as the initial state for the next, preventing excessive file sizes and memory overhead. In addition to configuring hot start options in Simulation Options, users can select File >> Export >> Hot Start File from the main menu at any time step during a run to save instantaneous nodal water depths, link discharge rates, and groundwater table elevations.

RDII Files

The RDII Interface File stores a time series of rainfall-dependent infiltration and inflow hydrographs for a specified set of drainage system nodes. In Avaris CS, RDII can be computed dynamically during a run using triangular Unit Hydrographs (RTK parameters) driven by rain gage data, or it can be directly supplied via an RDII interface file. RDII files generated internally by Avaris CS are saved in binary format, whereas RDII files created externally (e.g., from SCADA flow monitoring data or external sewer leakage models) are text files following the standard ASCII format used for routing interface files, with Flow as the sole variable.

Decoupling RDII generation from hydraulic pipe routing allows engineers to calibrate RTK unit hydrographs against field monitoring data independently and store the resulting flows in an RDII interface file. Reusing this interface file across various capital improvement scenarios ensures consistent, reproducible inflow boundary conditions, significantly improving the accuracy of sewer rehabilitation and storage basin capacity evaluations.

Routing Files

A Routing Interface File stores a time series of flow hydrographs and pollutant pollutographs discharged from the terminal outfall nodes of a drainage system model. This file acts as a dynamic hydraulic interface, providing inflow boundary conditions to downstream drainage models connected at those outfall locations.

When modeling large metropolitan sewer networks or regional watersheds, combining tens of thousands of conduits and subcatchments into a single model file creates massive computational overhead and complex data maintenance. Routing interface files enable a "divide-and-conquer" modular modeling strategy:

1.Upstream Sub-model Execution: The upstream catchment is modeled independently, saving discharge hydrographs and pollutographs at its outfalls into a routing interface file.

2.Downstream Sub-model Linkage: The downstream model designates this routing file as an inflow interface file (Inflow Routing File), applying the discharge time series directly as nodal inflows.

3.Interface File Combination: If a downstream node receives discharges from multiple independent upstream systems, users can utilize the Combine utility available in Avaris CS to merge multiple routing interface files into a single unified interface file.

This modular coupling methodology allows different engineering teams or municipal districts to construct and calibrate sub-models independently, linking them seamlessly into a comprehensive watershed-wide hydraulic and water quality model.

RDII/Routing File Format

In Avaris CS, text-based RDII interface files and routing interface files share an identical ASCII text format:

1.the keyword "AVARIS_DS" (without quotes) on the first line

2.a line of text describing the file context or simulation scenario (can be blank)

3.the recording time step utilized across all inflow records, expressed as integer seconds

4.the total number of stored variables, where the first variable must always be flow rate

5.the name and measurement units of each variable listed on separate lines, where the first variable is always FLOW

6.the number of nodes containing recorded time-series data

7.the unique identifier name of each node, listed one per line

8.a line of text providing column headings for the subsequent data table (can be blank)

9.for each node at each time step, a line containing:

  • the node identifier name
  • the date (year, month, and day separated by spaces)
  • the time of day (hours, minutes, and seconds separated by spaces)
  • the flow rate followed by the concentration value of each water quality constituent

Time periods during which no flow or pollutant loading occurs across any node can be omitted to optimize file storage. This clean, transparent text structure allows automated scripts and third-party software platforms to seamlessly parse, process, or synthesize Avaris CS interface files for custom modeling workflows and enterprise water platform integration.

Avaris CS 4.0.2

Function Description: Allows the user to dynamically play the simulation results on the map, visually displaying the temporal variations of flow, water level, or pollutant concentration.

Operation Steps: After the simulation is completed, click Simulation → Play (or click the Play button on the toolbar) to open the “Play Setting” windows, in which you can set the parameters to be displayed, such as Subcatchments, Links, Nodes, and Meshes. Click OK to display the corresponding legend on the map.

Function Description: Allows viewing of the simulation results for any selected object.

Operation Steps: After the simulation is completed, click on a visual object in the study area. The Property Browser will be displayed on the right side.

Editing tips

  • Type a value and press Enter, or leave the field. A numeric field that gets text shows a message and keeps the old value.
  • Rows with a pencil button (✎) open an editor: the Direct Inflows, Dry Weather Flows, Groundwater and LID Usages editors, the Cross Section Editor, the Curve Editor or the Time Series Editor. Next to a curve or time-series list, the pencil opens the chosen item; with <Empty> chosen it opens the right curve group so that you can create one. The list is refreshed when the editor closes.
  • Click a property name to filter the change Log by that variable.
  • With several objects selected, the heading Common Properties lists only the rows that every selected object of the chosen type shows. Rows with different values show *Multiple Values. Editor (pencil) rows are left out; use Batch Edit..., Open in Table, Zoom To or Delete below the grid.
  • After a run, Graph plots the results of the selected object and, for nodes and links, Long Section opens the profile tool.
  • Every change can be undone with Ctrl+Z.

Subcatchment

A subcatchment is a piece of land that drains to one point: a node of the network or another subcatchment. Rain from its rain gage falls on an impervious and a pervious sub-area. The model takes off depression storage, infiltration (pervious area only) and evaporation, and routes the excess as overland flow, treated as a non-linear reservoir, to the outlet. Optional groundwater, LID units and pollutant buildup are attached to the subcatchment.

Junction

A junction is a point where links meet: a manhole, a chamber or a confluence of channels. It has no storage volume of its own apart from a small surface area. When the water level rises above the rim plus the surcharge depth, the excess leaves the network as flooding: it is lost, or it ponds on the surface and returns later when ponding is allowed.

Conduit

A conduit is a pipe or an open channel that carries water from its upstream to its downstream node. Flow is computed from its cross section, length, roughness (Manning's equation, or Hazen-Williams / Darcy-Weisbach for force mains) and the water levels at both ends. Its invert at each end is the node invert plus an offset.

Pump

A pump is a link that lifts water from its inlet node to its outlet node. Its flow is given by a pump curve, as a function of the wet-well volume, the inlet depth or the head across the pump, and it switches on and off by the water depth at the inlet node or by control rules.

Orifice

An orifice is an opening in the wall or floor of a chamber or tank, such as a throttle or a gate. Flow through it depends on the area of the opening, the discharge coefficient and the head on it; when the opening is only partly submerged it behaves like a weir. Control rules can change its opening.

An outlet is a flow control device whose discharge is given by a rating relation: a power function or a table of outflow against the water depth at the inlet node, or against the head difference across the device. Use it for vortex valves, throttles and other devices with a known head–discharge curve, or for outflow from a storage unit.

Map Label

A map label is a piece of text placed on the map, for example a street or area name. It is not part of the drainage model and does not affect the simulation. Labels are saved in the [LABELS] section of the .inp file.

Fields

The Properties pane shows the heading Map Label and the label text only; there are no editable rows.

PropertyMeaning
(text under the heading)The text of the label.

Editing tips

  • Right-click the label on the map and choose Edit Label Text... to change the text, or Delete Label to remove it.
  • Drag the label to move it.
  • Font, size and anchor read from an .inp file are kept when the model is saved.
  • Labels are left out when several objects are selected in the Properties pane.
